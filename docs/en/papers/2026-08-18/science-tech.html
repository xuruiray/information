Space, Energy & Robotics Briefing

In space and energy, Webb observations of a compact, bright red core resembling early-universe ‘little red dots’ suggest they may hide full galaxies. Graphene wrinkles were found to create strong charge separation, offering a way to tune electronics at the atomic scale. Webb’s Mysterious Little Red Dots May Be Hiding Entire Galaxies ScienceDaily 科学

Astronomers may finally have a clue to the fate of the mysterious ‘little red dots’ that crowded the early universe. By studying a spiral galaxy nicknamed the ‘Saguaro,’ they found a compact, bright red center closely resembling those distant objects—but surrounded by a full galaxy that Webb usually cannot see.

Read more → Tiny Graphene Wrinkles Create Surprisingly Powerful Electrical Effects ScienceDaily 技术

Scientists found that tiny, sharply curved wrinkles in graphene can dramatically alter its electrical behavior, creating surprisingly strong charge separation. The finding suggests future electronics could be tuned by reshaping materials at the atomic scale instead of changing their composition, potentially enabling new energy conversion and device concepts.

Scientific Research Briefing

In scientific research, scientists are searching ancient rocks for 1.7-billion-year-old microfossils that could reveal how complex life began. An eight-year Finnish study found children’s screen time was associated with better cognitive processing in adolescence, challenging assumptions. Animals possess enzymes for digesting natural bioplastics, and an experimental compound blocked Alzheimer’s-related protein clumps in mice, suggesting a new therapeutic approach.

Tiny 1.7-Billion-Year-Old Fossils Could Reveal How Complex Life Began ScienceDaily 科学

Scientists are searching some of Earth's oldest rocks for tiny fossils that could reveal how simple microbial life made the extraordinary leap toward plants, animals, and other complex organisms. Solving that ancient mystery may also help determine whether complex life could emerge on worlds beyond Earth.

Read more → Scientists Tracked Kids for 8 Years — The Screen Time Result Was Unexpected ScienceDaily 科学

An eight-year Finnish study found that children who spent more time on screens tended to show better cognitive processing as teenagers, challenging common assumptions about screen use. Researchers say the key may be balancing physical activity with screen activities that encourage learning, creativity, and active thinking.

Read more → Animals Have Been Eating Nature’s Original Bioplastic for Millions of Years ScienceDaily 科学

Researchers discovered that dozens of animal species possess enzymes capable of breaking down microbial bioplastics called PHAs, a talent once thought to belong only to microorganisms. Nature invented biodegradable plastic long before humans, and animals may have been feeding on it for hundreds of millions of years.

Read more → A Little-Known Protein May Be Fueling Alzheimer’s — And Scientists Found a Way to Block It ScienceDaily 科学

The treatment also appeared to improve heart health and slow some signs of aging, raising hopes for an entirely new way to combat dementia.
