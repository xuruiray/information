Scientific Research Briefing

This subsection covers four studies: AP503 strengthened bones in mice via GPR133; semaglutide was linked to nearly 40% fewer asthma attacks; astrobiologists remained skeptical of 2025 alien-life signals; and interstellar comet 3I/ATLAS is rich in methanol, suggesting a distinctive formation environment. The selection highlights advances in bone health, respiratory outcomes, astrobiology, and interstellar chemistry.

Scientists find a bone-building switch that could fight osteoporosis ScienceDaily 科学

An experimental compound called AP503 significantly strengthened bones in mice by activating GPR133, a receptor that boosts bone formation while slowing bone loss. Because the same treatment has also been linked to stronger muscles, researchers see potential for combating age-related decline and osteoporosis.

Read more → Ozempic and Wegovy linked to nearly 40% fewer asthma attacks ScienceDaily 科学

A large real-world study linked semaglutide, the drug in Ozempic and Wegovy, to nearly 40% fewer asthma attacks. It was also associated with about 20% fewer COPD flare-ups. Researchers say GLP-1 drugs may offer respiratory benefits beyond diabetes and obesity, but clinical trials are needed to confirm the findings.

Read more → Have we found alien life? Hundreds of scientists weigh in ScienceDaily 科学

Two major alien-life announcements in 2025 generated enormous excitement, but most astrobiologists remained unconvinced. Only 6.6% of surveyed experts thought possible biological molecules on K2-18b probably represented extraterrestrial life, while 15.1% said the same about intriguing mineral patterns in a Martian rock.

Read more → Interstellar comet 3I/ATLAS is bursting with methanol ScienceDaily 科学

Interstellar comet 3I/ATLAS contains exceptionally high levels of methanol, suggesting it formed under conditions very different from those that shaped most comets in our solar system. ALMA also revealed that tiny icy grains around the comet are releasing methanol like miniature comets, giving astronomers an unusually detailed look at an object from another star system.
