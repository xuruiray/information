The secret to growing food on Mars could be fungi ScienceDaily 技术

Scientists think beneficial fungi could help turn nutrient-poor lunar and Martian regolith into material capable of supporting crops. By helping plants access nutrients and withstand harsh conditions, these microbes could someday make self-sustaining space farming more realistic. The approach could reduce reliance on Earth-supplied soil and fertilizer for long-duration missions.

Scientific Research Briefing

This scientific research subsection features four advances: Artemis II astronauts transition to emeritus roles at NASA while advising future lunar missions; REM sleep shows an energy paradox in which blood supply rises but neuronal ATP falls; a single CRISPR therapy halved LDL cholesterol and triglycerides for a year; and glucosamine use is linked to faster progression from mild cognitive impairment to dementia.

Two astronauts who flew farther than anyone before are taking new NASA roles ScienceDaily 科学

Artemis II astronauts Victor Glover and Reid Wiseman are moving to emeritus status at NASA after their historic lunar flyaround earlier this year. They will continue advising and mentoring NASA teams as the agency accelerates preparations for Artemis III and future lunar missions. Their Orion and deep-space experience is expected to be especially valuable.

Read more → REM sleep paradox: Dreaming may drain the brain’s energy even as fuel supply rises ScienceDaily 科学

Scientists found an energy paradox in the brain during REM sleep, the stage linked to dreaming. Blood supply rises before REM begins, but neurons’ immediately available energy, measured as ATP, then drops. The finding suggests dreaming may require intense internal processing that drains energy.

Read more → Glucosamine, a popular joint supplement, linked to faster Alzheimer’s progression ScienceDaily 科学

A large health-record analysis found glucosamine use was associated with a 25% higher likelihood of mild cognitive impairment progressing to dementia. Lab experiments suggest the supplement may intensify abnormal sugar-tagging in Alzheimer’s brains, though clinical trials are needed to confirm whether it affects disease progression.

Read more → One CRISPR treatment cut “bad” cholesterol in half for a full year ScienceDaily 科学

A single dose of an experimental CRISPR therapy cut LDL cholesterol and triglycerides by about half in patients with difficult-to-treat lipid disorders. Reductions persisted after one year, with the highest dose lowering LDL by 52.5% and triglycerides by 47.8%. The result points to durable gene-editing benefits for chronic cardiovascular risk.
