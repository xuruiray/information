This edition of the AI & LLMs section focuses on evaluation and reliability. Frontier work includes a multi-institutional bioengineering benchmark, a scope-compliance test for autonomous agents, hierarchical memory for agent teams and a survey on fake review detection. Frontier Models

Frontier work here centres on evaluation and reliability. BioEVAL extends large-model benchmarking to frontier bioengineering tasks, ScopeBench tests whether autonomous agents stay inside authorized engagement boundaries, and a hierarchical memory design targets validity-aware retrieval in agent teams. A survey tracks fake review detection from pre-trained to large models, while Cartograph addresses tool discovery at MCP scale.

BioEVAL: A global, multi-institutional benchmark of large language and multimodal models for bioengineering arXiv 人工智能

A multi-institutional team introduces BioEVAL, a benchmark of large language and multimodal models for bioengineering tasks. It targets frontier and multimodal capabilities rather than factual recall, offering a standardised way to compare models on scientific problem solving across research groups. The authors argue existing benchmarks under-represent such frontier work.

Read more → ScopeBench: Do Agents Preserve Engagement Boundaries Under Goal Pressure? arXiv 人工智能

ScopeBench evaluates autonomous agents on whether they stay within a client's authorized engagement scope during web and network penetration testing. With offensive-security benchmarks saturating, the authors argue that boundary compliance, not raw hacking skill, is now the main barrier to deployment. The benchmark is designed for real autonomous deployments.

Read more → Not All Memories Are Equal: Hierarchical Collaborative Memory for Validity-Aware Retrieval in LLM Agents arXiv 计算语言学

The paper proposes a hierarchical collaborative memory scheme that separates team-level decisions and consensus from individual observations and execution traces, then performs validity-aware retrieval. The goal is to improve how LLM agents recall and use information in team settings. It treats memory as heterogeneous and evolving rather than a single store.

Read more → A Survey on Fake Review Detection: From Pre-trained Language Models to Large Language Models arXiv 计算语言学

This survey reviews fake review detection from pre-trained language models to LLMs. It notes that large models cut both ways: they improve detection, but also make generating deceptive reviews far cheaper, raising new challenges for platforms, rating systems and public trust. The authors map research directions for detection under generative models.

Read more → Cartograph: Federated Tool Discovery with Operator-Attested Retrieval for AI Agents arXiv 计算语言学

As Model Context Protocol catalogues grow, Cartograph acts as a federated MCP proxy that turns agent-visible tool discovery from full catalog traversal into progressive disclosure, cutting loading cost while adding operator-attested retrieval. The authors frame this as changing discovery complexity from O(n) traversal to O(k) progressive disclosure.

Research & Governance

Research & Governance

This subsection covers explainability evaluation, agent security and benchmark standardisation. A synthetic ground-truth framework gives XAI methods a verifiable reference, skill cascading attacks expose compositional risks in third-party skill ecosystems, and Benchy plus a systematic-review screening framework push benchmarking toward standardised and domain-specific settings. A structure-based protein topology study also shows AI methods moving deeper into scientific domains.

A Synthetic Ground-Truth Framework for the Evaluation of Explainable AI Methods arXiv 人工智能

Evaluating explainable AI has been hampered by the lack of ground-truth explanations. The paper proposes a synthetic ground-truth framework that provides verifiable references, allowing more systematic measurement of explanation fidelity and reliability. It aims to replace ad hoc fidelity checks with a controlled reference setting.

Read more → Stealth Apart, Harm Together: Skill Cascading Attacks on Skill-Based Agent Systems arXiv 人工智能

Skills are loadable packages of instructions and scripts whose openness enables reuse but also risk. The paper introduces skill cascading attacks, showing how individually benign skills can combine into harmful behaviour in agent systems. It highlights supply-chain style risks in open, third-party skill ecosystems used by agents.

Read more → Benchy: towards a universal language for task-oriented AI benchmarks arXiv 人工智能

Benchy proposes a semantic language and execution engine for AI benchmarking. A benchmark is defined as a program, scoring function and dataset, separate from the system under test, making benchmarks easier to author, share and reproduce. The paper presents it as a step toward a universal benchmark specification.

Read more → A Benchmark Framework for Screening Automation in Systematic Reviews arXiv 计算语言学

Screening literature for systematic reviews is labor-intensive, and LLMs could help classify relevance. The paper proposes a benchmark framework for comparing model performance on this task in a more consistent way. It targets the screening phase, often a major bottleneck in evidence synthesis and review production.

Read more → Predicting Transmembrane Protein Topology from 3D Structure arXiv 人工智能

Using the graph neural network SchNet, the paper infers transmembrane protein topology from 3D structure rather than sequence alone, trained and validated with five-fold cross-validation on the same dataset used for DeepTMHMM. The work compares a structure-based route with established sequence-based predictors such as DeepTMHMM.
