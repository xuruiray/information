Research & Governance

This briefing highlights PoolBench, a standardized benchmark for pooling strategies in decoder-only LLM evaluation; an LLM-based multidimensional analysis of political news; and a study warning that large language models could threaten double-blind peer review. The items offer both new evaluation tools and governance perspectives.

PoolBench: A Benchmark for Pooling Strategies in Concept Representation Evaluation for Decoder-Only LLMs arXiv 计算语言学

PoolBench is a new benchmark for evaluating pooling strategies in concept representation work with decoder-only LLMs. Because practitioners must collapse token-level hidden states into passage-level vectors but lack a shared protocol, the benchmark aims to standardize comparisons across concepts, models, and tasks, reducing confounded results.

Read more → Large Language Models Threaten Double-blind Review arXiv 计算语言学

A new study warns that large language models could undermine double-blind peer review. Anonymized manuscripts may be deanonymized by models via citation networks, stylistic fingerprints, or similar cues, weakening protection against status and affiliation bias. The authors urge the scientific community to take the risk seriously.

Read more → Beyond Sentiment: Comparing Traditional NLP and LLM-Based Multi-Dimensional Analysis for Political News Evaluation arXiv 计算语言学

The paper compares traditional sentiment analysis with LLM-based multidimensional analysis for evaluating political news. It argues that LLMs capture rhetorical, ideological, and framing dimensions beyond simple polarity, making them more suitable for social-science and humanities research on complex political discourse.
