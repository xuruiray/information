Some signs of quantum gravity may be an illusion ScienceDaily 技术

Quantum mechanics and Einstein's theory of gravity explain nearly everything we see in nature, yet physicists still do not know how to unite them. A new theoretical framework suggests that some experiments that appear to show gravity behaving quantum mechanically may have a much more ordinary explanation. The researchers argue that certain presumed gravitational quantum effects could actually arise from classical interactions, adding an important caveat to ongoing efforts to observe quantum gravity directly.

Read more → IBM quantum computer solves classically intractable problem in 15 minutes ScienceDaily 技术

IBM and University of Chicago researchers have completed a quantum computation that leading classical methods could not practically reproduce. The system used 70 error-corrected logical qubits and finished the task in roughly 15 minutes while also providing statistical evidence that the result was reliable. This achievement represents a significant step toward error-corrected quantum computing and shows that quantum systems can outperform classical simulations on specific problems.

Read more → Scientists turn one of the hardest plastics to recycle into high-performance engine lubricant ScienceDaily 技术

Researchers have discovered a way to turn notoriously difficult-to-recycle PVC plastic into a key ingredient used in high-performance lubricants such as engine oil. The technique could give mountains of plastic waste a valuable second life while making lubricant production more sustainable. By converting a common plastic waste stream into a useful material, the method addresses both environmental pollution and resource efficiency.

Read more → The universe is still speeding up, but nobody knows why ScienceDaily 技术

A major new analysis challenges recent claims that the universe's expansion is slowing, finding that the evidence for cosmic acceleration remains strong. The findings preserve the leading role of dark energy, while leaving scientists with the enduring mystery of what this strange cosmic force actually is. The study reinforces the standard cosmological model and highlights the need for new probes to understand dark energy's nature.

Scientific Research Briefing

Scientific research in this edition spans spacetime crystals, clean energy, paleoanthropology and planetary science. Physicists show a spacetime crystal could collapse into a microscopic black hole; Australia's red soil may produce hydrogen at scale; 1.4-million-year-old footprints reveal large human relatives; Mars harbors a massive internal heat anomaly. These findings open new questions in physics, geology and human evolution.

This strange “spacetime crystal” can suddenly become a black hole ScienceDaily 科学

Physicists have mathematically captured a bizarre "spacetime crystal" that can either dissolve or collapse into a microscopic black hole after only a tiny change in energy. Their breakthrough came from an unusual trick involving infinitely many dimensions, potentially giving researchers a new tool for studying primordial and microscopic black holes. The work also offers fresh insights into the interplay between quantum mechanics and general relativity.

Read more → Australia’s red soil may be hiding a massive clean energy source ScienceDaily 科学

Researchers have discovered that Western Australia's iron-rich rocks can naturally generate hydrogen and that the process may be boosted to produce much larger amounts. If it can be scaled up, the vast formations beneath the Pilbara could become a major new source of low-emission energy for Australia and potentially the world. The finding opens the door to a new class of natural hydrogen resources with significant economic and environmental implications.

Read more → 1.4-million-year-old footprints reveal a surprisingly large human relative ScienceDaily 科学

Ancient footprints in Kenya reveal that eight Paranthropus boisei may have traveled together 1.4 million years ago, with some individuals reaching surprisingly large, human-like body sizes. The tracks offer a rare glimpse of their social behavior and suggest these extinct human relatives may have lived in more complex groups than previously thought. The discovery challenges previous assumptions about the size and social organization of early hominins.

Read more → Scientists discover a massive heat anomaly deep inside Mars ScienceDaily 科学

Mars has a hidden temperature divide, with its southern interior potentially hundreds of degrees hotter than the north and partially molten. The discovery could help explain several long-standing Martian mysteries, including unusual magnetism, seismic behavior, and clues to the planet's ancient water history. Understanding this internal heat anomaly is crucial for reconstructing Mars' geological evolution and its past habitability.
