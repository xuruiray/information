Frontier Models Briefing

This subsection covers recent model, training and evaluation work: the Pistis release of 27B and 9B multimodal models, studies on LLM politeness judgments and argumentative defences against character attacks, and dual-entropy policy optimization for multimodal hallucination.

Polite but Misaligned: LLM Politeness Judgments Diverge from Human Pragmatic Norms arXiv 计算语言学

A new paper evaluates whether large language models judge politeness in ways that align with human pragmatic norms. Using two English-language datasets with complementary annotation formats, including continuous human ratings, the authors find that strong benchmark performance does not guarantee alignment with human social-pragmatic judgments.

Read more → Pistis Technical Report Introduces 27B and 9B Multimodal Model Family arXiv 人工智能

The Pistis model family comprises 27B- and 9B-parameter multimodal large language models built on Qwen3.6 and Qwen3.5. Developed with a general and scalable post-training framework, it first builds a strong foundation through large-scale multimodal supervision before further optimization.

Read more → BaseCamp: An Agentic AI Framework for Automating DNA Sequencing Pipelines arXiv 人工智能

DNA sequencing pipelines covering quality control, alignment, variant calling and annotation can already run at scale via workflow systems, yet the decision layer around execution remains manual, from selecting quality thresholds onward. BaseCamp proposes an agentic AI framework to automate that decision layer.

Read more → Benchmarking Argumentative Behaviour of LLMs: Defences Against Character Attacks arXiv 计算语言学

As large language models are increasingly deployed as argumentative agents in persuasive dialogues, rigorous evaluation of their debating competence is needed. This study focuses on character attacks, or ad hominem arguments, traditionally dismissed as fallacies, and benchmarks how LLMs defend against them.

Read more → DEEPO: Dual-Entropy Enhanced Policy Optimization for Hallucination in MLLMs arXiv 人工智能

Reinforcement learning is widely used to sharpen reasoning in multimodal large language models, yet its effect on hallucination is uneven. The paper traces this to two weak points in the correction chain from reward to parameter update and proposes Dual-Entropy Enhanced Policy Optimization to address them.

Research & Governance Briefing

This subsection focuses on evaluation, safety and governance: TW3Cast ranks near the top on GIFT-Eval, alongside explainable hate-speech detection, policy-driven agent simulation, uncertainty quantification for time-series forecasting, and access control and structural governance for tool-using agents.

TW3Cast: A Frozen Router of Lightly Fine-Tuned Foundation Models for Time-Series Forecasting arXiv 人工智能

TW3Cast is a time-series forecasting system that reached third place among 130 entries on the GIFT-Eval benchmark by mean MASE rank as of September 14, 2026. The two entries above it belong to the leaderboard's agentic category, multi-step systems that use agents or language models to reason about, generate or select forecasts.

Read more → Explainable DistilBERT-BiLSTM-Attention Framework for Binary and Multi-Class Hate Speech Detection arXiv 计算语言学

Hate speech on social media poses risks to social harmony, mental well-being and public safety, making timely and accurate detection essential for content moderation. Most existing studies focus on binary classification, evaluate on a single dataset and provide limited interpretability; this work proposes an explainable framework for binary and multi-class detection.

Read more → PAWS: Policy-driven Agentic World Simulation arXiv 人工智能

Policy interventions propagate through public communication, institutional decisions and stakeholder responses, yet datasets for financial multi-agent simulation rarely connect these processes with temporally aligned historical evidence. PAWS introduces a policy-driven agentic world simulation dataset covering such processes.

Read more → SGA: Uncertainty Quantification for Multi-Step Forecasting in Time Series Foundation Models arXiv 机器学习

Time-series foundation models have significantly advanced multi-step forecasting, enabling accurate predictions over extended horizons. However, existing models often suffer from inherent uncertainty, typically manifesting as distorted forecast intervals. The paper proposes SGA for uncertainty quantification in multi-step forecasting.

Read more → Progressive Skill Discovery as Access Control for Tool-Using LLM Agents arXiv 人工智能

LLM agents struggle to scale safely when exposed to vast enterprise toolsets. Giving an agent access to every internal tool leads to oversized context windows, degraded tool selection and severe governance vulnerabilities, since policies defined purely in prompts remain unreliable. The paper proposes progressive skill discovery as role-scoped capability delivery for structural governance.
