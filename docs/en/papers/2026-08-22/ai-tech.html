AI & LLMs Briefing

This edition of AI & LLMs focuses on frontier model research and governance. On models, multimodal LLMs extend to industrial remaining-useful-life prediction and dynamic ECG analysis; hallucination is reframed as a tool for scientific hypothesis generation; system-message adherence is systematically benchmarked; and active inference offers a new way for agents to acquire context. On governance, a bilingual Nepali-English benchmark for out-of-context misinformation, black-box LLM safety detection, agent-ready scientific data services, bioinformatics software entity recognition, and empirical retraining policies for streaming ML together show a landscape balancing safety and practicality.

Frontier Models

Frontier model research makes multi-pronged progress: multimodal LLMs expand to time-series and medical signals, hallucination is recast as a source of scientific creativity, system-message adherence gains a dedicated benchmark, and active inference provides an optimal trade-off framework for agent context acquisition. These works highlight both new capabilities and new evaluation methods.

Time-Series Retrieval for Grounding Multimodal Language Models in Remaining Useful Life arXiv 计算语言学

This paper investigates whether large language models and agentic AI systems can effectively support prognostics and health management. It proposes time-series retrieval to ground multimodal language models in remaining useful life estimation, offering a new technical approach for equipment health management. The approach integrates retrieval mechanisms to improve grounding and reliability in maintenance tasks.

Read more → Holtercare-Bench: A Multimodal Benchmark for Evaluating Long-Term Dynamic ECG Analysis arXiv 机器学习

Holtercare-Bench is a multimodal benchmark for long-term dynamic ECG analysis, evaluating multimodal large language models on complex temporal reasoning and diagnostic report generation. Results suggest that current models still struggle with this critical medical task, highlighting the need for high-quality datasets.

Read more → Hallucination as a Feature, not a Defect: Evaluating a multi-agent architecture to transform speculative language-model outputs into testable scientific hypotheses arXiv 计算语言学

This work reframes hallucination as a source of combinatorial creativity rather than a defect. It evaluates a multi-agent architecture that converts speculative language-model outputs into testable scientific hypotheses, challenging the prevailing alignment approach that solely suppresses hallucination.

Read more → Compliance, Capability, and Conflict: Benchmarking Multimodal LLMs under System Messages arXiv 计算语言学

This benchmark evaluates how multimodal large language models adhere to system messages. It finds that existing evaluations mostly place constraints in text or user turns, leaving system-message adherence in multimodal contexts largely unmeasured, and reveals conflicts between compliance and capability.

Read more → Active Inference as Context Acquisition for AI Agents arXiv 人工智能

The paper formulates context acquisition as an active inference problem for interactive AI agents. When users omit constraints, preferences, or files, the agent must decide between default assumptions and costly clarification or retrieval, and the proposed framework optimizes this tradeoff for efficient interaction.

Research & Governance

Research & Governance

Governance research advances on multiple fronts: a bilingual OOC benchmark fills a gap for Nepali; black-box safety detection offers low-cost identification of harmful outputs; scientific data services become agent-ready; bioinformatics software recognition assists literature mining; and retraining policy studies provide guidance for production ML deployment.

NepOOC-M: Bilingual Nepali-English Benchmark and Comparative Analysis of Multimodal Architectures for OOC Detection arXiv 计算语言学

Out-of-context misinformation pairs authentic images with misleading captions without image manipulation, making detection a problem of multimodal alignment. The authors introduce NepOOC-M, a bilingual Nepali-English benchmark, and comparatively analyze multimodal architectures, finding that current models need better alignment to address this issue.

Read more → Enforcing LLM Safety through DMD-based Classification of Prompt-Response Embedding Dynamics arXiv 人工智能

For high-stakes applications, the paper extends a recent method to detect unsafe LLM outputs in a black-box manner. It uses dynamic mode decomposition to classify prompt-response embedding dynamics, efficiently identifying toxic, harmful, or policy-violating content without white-box access.

Read more → Scientific Data Skills: Enabling Agent-Ready Scientific Data Services at Scale arXiv 人工智能

The paper proposes 'scientific data skills' to make datasets more accessible to AI agents across heterogeneous repositories. It aims to enable automatic discovery, interpretation, and invocation of scientific data, supporting autonomous research workflows at scale.

Read more → Automatic bioinformatic software named entity recognition from literature arXiv 计算语言学

Bioinformatics software and databases are essential to life science research, but their mentions in literature are often inconsistent and hard to identify at scale. This study develops automatic named entity recognition to build a comprehensive, up-to-date catalog of bioinformatics resources, aiding systematic literature mining.

Read more → When to Retrain: An Empirical Study of Retraining Policies for Streaming ML Under Concept Drift, Budget, and Latency Constraints arXiv 机器学习

Production ML systems degrade under concept drift, yet practitioners have little guidance on when to retrain. This empirical study compares retraining policies under budget and latency constraints, showing that training and deployment latency leaves stale models serving predictions and that more principled retraining strategies are needed.
