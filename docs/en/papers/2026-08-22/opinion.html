The community this week is buzzing over three provocative items: an accidental logging of phone calls to military bases raises security questions; a citizen faces felony charges for deleting data at the US border, stirring debate over state power and privacy; and an essay on becoming 'AI-blind' reflects on how technology reshapes human perception.

I accidentally logged hundreds of thousands of phone calls to military bases Hacker News

The author recounts an accidental discovery: a technical exploration that resulted in the logging of hundreds of thousands of phone calls intended for military bases. This unexpected incident highlights glaring weaknesses in telephone numbering infrastructure and raises urgent questions about privacy, security, and the disclosure of vulnerabilities.

Read more → Felony charges for citizen deleting phone data at US Border Hacker News

A US citizen now faces federal felony charges for deleting phone data at a US border crossing. The case has ignited a heated debate over the limits of border enforcement and the protection of personal privacy, underscoring the enduring tension between state authority and civil liberties.

Read more → I'm becoming AI-blind Hacker News

In a reflective essay, the author describes growing numbness toward AI-generated content and a creeping inability to distinguish machine output from human writing. The piece examines how this 'AI blindness' might affect perception, trust and creativity, and whether such familiarity leads to complacency or critical insight.
