3 articles
Research & Governance

The Research & Governance subsection includes three papers. They examine how LLMs diverge from humans in linguistic uncertainty quantification, introduce SW-KAN as a new network architecture, and analyze model-dependent mastery thresholds in knowledge tracing. Topics span trustworthy AI, model architectures, and educational AI governance.

"very likely" Means "uncertain"? How LLMs Diverge from Humans in Linguistic Uncertainty Quantification arXiv 机器学习

Humans express uncertainty verbally using markers such as 'possible' or 'likely', but most LLM uncertainty quantification relies on likelihood- or consistency-based signals. From a cognitive perspective, accurate verbal uncertainty reflects metacognitive monitoring and knowledge boundaries. This paper examines how LLMs diverge from humans in linguistic uncertainty quantification.

Read more → SW-KAN: Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks with Stieltjes-Wigert q-Orthogonal Polynomials arXiv 机器学习

Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KANs) replace fixed node activations with learnable univariate functions on edges, offering enhanced interpretability and parameter efficiency. This paper introduces SW-KAN, which uses Stieltjes-Wigert q-orthogonal polynomials. The work addresses computational overhead in recent polynomial-based KAN variants.

Read more → One Mastery Threshold Does Not Fit All Knowledge Tracing Models arXiv 机器学习

Tutoring systems use mastery thresholds to decide when students can stop practicing and advance. However, the same numerical threshold can lead to very different decisions when the underlying knowledge tracing model changes. This study examines six knowledge tracing models across four public educational datasets and evaluates 12 thresholds.
