Scientific Research Briefing

Four studies stand out. A proposed superfluid-helium qubit could cut quantum error rates by about 100 times. Scientists identified two new large Australian stick insect species and corrected a decades-long misidentification. A new “Plant Armor” fabric more than tripled strawberry yields, and closer adherence to the MIND diet was linked to slower brain aging, equivalent to about 2.5 years.

This new qubit could be 100 times less error-prone in superfluid quantum computer breakthrough ScienceDaily 科学

A proposed qubit made with superfluid helium could cut quantum computing error rates by about 100 times by shielding quantum information from common electromagnetic noise. If experiments confirm the predictions, the technology could eventually work alongside today's superconducting qubits or serve as a new kind of quantum memory.

Read more → This giant stick insect fooled scientists for decades ScienceDaily 科学

Scientists have identified two new species of large Australian stick insects, including one that lays unusual “lidded” eggs never before recorded in the group. The work also showed that a familiar stick insect had been misidentified for decades, highlighting how much biodiversity may still be hidden in plain sight.

Read more → New “Plant Armor” more than triples strawberry yields ScienceDaily 科学

A new protective fabric boosted strawberry yields by as much as 3.56 times while blocking insects and still allowing sunlight, rain and airflow to reach the plants. Originally inspired by research on military clothing, Plant Armor could also help farms reduce pesticide and water use.

Read more → This diet may slow brain aging by 2.5 years ScienceDaily 科学

People who followed the MIND diet more closely showed slower brain shrinkage and less grey matter loss over time, with the strongest differences equivalent to about 2.5 years of delayed brain aging. Berries and poultry were linked with healthier brain changes, while sweets and fried fast foods were associated with greater tissue loss.
