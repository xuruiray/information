Scientific Research Briefing

This edition's scientific research coverage focuses on AI-driven materials science, food safety risks, human impacts on wildlife, and astrophysics. Researchers used AI to optimise 3D-printing parameters for a NASA alloy; all tested plant-based meats in the UK contained fungal toxins; tracking data from thousands of animals showed human presence affects movement as much as infrastructure; and astronomers captured the first clear image of Betelgeuse's companion.

AI searched 100 million possibilities and found a cheaper way to 3D-print a NASA rocket alloy ScienceDaily 科学

Researchers used AI to search through more than 100 million possible settings for 3D-printing a high-performance NASA alloy. After only 40 experiments, the system identified six successful configurations, including one that worked at a record-low 500 watts. That could allow GRCop-42, currently difficult and expensive to print, to be made with much more widely available equipment.

Read more → Scientists tested 212 plant-based meat alternatives. Every one contained fungal toxins ScienceDaily 科学

Every one of 212 plant-based meat alternatives and drinks tested in the UK contained at least one naturally occurring fungal toxin, though concentrations remained below recommended EU guideline levels. Researchers say the bigger question is whether regularly eating large amounts of these foods could lead to potentially concerning cumulative exposure over time.

Read more → Scientists tracked 4,500 animals and found a surprising human effect ScienceDaily 科学

Scientists who tracked 4,500 animals found that our physical presence can reshape where wildlife moves and lives, sometimes as strongly as roads, cities, and other human development. Because different species reacted in very different ways, conservation efforts may need to become much more targeted.

Read more → "I jumped from my chair" – Astronomers spot Betelgeuse's hidden companion ScienceDaily 科学

In a new study, astronomers have captured their clearest image yet of Betelgeuse's long-suspected companion, potentially ending a search that lasted about 100 years. The surprisingly massive hidden star could help explain Betelgeuse's changing brightness and may influence its future supernova.
