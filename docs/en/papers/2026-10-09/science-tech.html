Scientific Research Briefing

This Scientific Research subsection covers paleoanthropology, lunar exploration, drug repurposing and genetics: South African fossils push Homo erectus back about 200,000 years; NASA seeks lunar caves and ice for a Moon base; an epilepsy drug shows potential against osteoarthritis; and a newly found protist breaks genetic code conventions.

Scientists found Homo erectus 200,000 years earlier than expected ScienceDaily 科学

New fossils from South Africa’s Drimolen Cave include an adult Homo erectus skull fragment dated to about 2 million years ago. Researchers say the finds suggest Homo erectus appeared in southern Africa roughly 200,000 years earlier than previously thought, reshaping early human evolution.

Read more → NASA is searching for hidden caves to build its first Moon base ScienceDaily 科学

NASA is preparing for humanity’s first Moon base with three investigations that will hunt for underground shelters, hidden ice, and hazardous lunar surface conditions. The findings could show how astronauts can survive and thrive on the Moon, laying groundwork for future Mars journeys.

Read more → FDA-approved epilepsy drug may help reverse osteoarthritis damage ScienceDaily 科学

Yale researchers found that lacosamide, an FDA-approved epilepsy drug, blocks a protein involved in both pain signaling and cartilage destruction. In preclinical studies, the drug helped damaged joints shift toward repair, suggesting a new osteoarthritis approach beyond pain relief.
