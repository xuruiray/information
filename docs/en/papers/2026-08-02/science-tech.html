Space, Energy & Robotics: This Edition

This edition of Space, Energy & Robotics highlights four striking advances: retaining water in sodium-ion batteries nearly doubles their power while aiding desalination; the world's first photonic time crystal allows unprecedented control of terahertz light; unusual gamma rays from zinc-70 illuminate how stars forge heavy elements; and a nanoscale cobalt-aluminum alloy achieves six to ten times the strength of structural steel while remaining flexible. Together, these reports showcase the cross-disciplinary nature of modern engineering and materials science.

Scientists left water inside a battery and nearly doubled its power ScienceDaily 技术

Researchers at the University of Surrey found that leaving naturally occurring water inside sodium vanadium oxide during battery manufacturing can nearly double power output. The simple change could also support efforts to turn seawater into drinking water, offering a low-cost route to better batteries and water desalination.

Read more → World-first photonic time crystal opens a new era of light control ScienceDaily 技术

Scientists have built the world's first all-optical photonic time crystal, enabling rapid control of terahertz light behavior. This breakthrough could lead to ultrafast computers, smarter communication networks, advanced imaging techniques, and a new generation of highly tunable lasers, opening a new era for light manipulation.

Read more → Strange gamma rays could reveal how stars forge heavy elements ScienceDaily 技术

Scientists traced an unexpected surge of low-energy gamma rays from zinc-70 to magnetic changes inside its nucleus. This important finding could refine models of how heavy elements are forged in stars, supernovae, and neutron star mergers, deepening our understanding of the cosmos's elemental abundance.

Read more → This new alloy is up to 10 times stronger than steel and surprisingly flexible ScienceDaily 技术

Engineers transformed a brittle cobalt-aluminum compound into a material that is both exceptionally strong and flexible. The nanoscale design achieves yield strength six to ten times that of high-strength structural steel while allowing substantial deformation at room temperature, opening new avenues for lightweight, ultra-strong structural materials.

Scientific Research: This Edition

This edition's science reports take readers from the Curiosity rover's honeycomb terrain on Mars to black holes expelling matter, primordial black holes triggering supernovae, and two 'ghost' ancestors hidden in our DNA. Spanning planetary geology, high-energy astrophysics, and human evolution, these discoveries illustrate the breadth and depth of contemporary scientific inquiry.

Black holes may spit out nearly as much matter as they swallow ScienceDaily 科学

Observations of a black hole during a dramatic 2023 eruption show that it did not simply devour gas from its companion star. It also expelled large amounts of material through powerful jets and winds, even after the outburst nearly faded, suggesting black holes may reshape their surroundings long after their brightest fireworks end.

Read more → Tiny black holes may be secretly exploding stars across the Milky Way ScienceDaily 科学

Primordial black holes may occasionally pass through white dwarf stars, triggering enormous Type Ia supernova explosions. Researchers say such events could explain chemical patterns seen in supernova remnants, nearby explosions, and stars across the Milky Way, providing a new window into dark matter and stellar evolution.

Modern humans carry DNA from two mysterious extinct lineages in addition to Neanderthals and Denisovans. This hidden genetic legacy suggests that our evolutionary past was shaped by repeated mixing among many different human groups, and adds new pages to the intricate story of human origins.
