In Space, Energy & Robotics, we feature two frontier developments. Strange quantum experiment shows 'negative time' is more than an illusion ScienceDaily 科学

Photons traveling through a cloud of atoms can emerge so early that they appear to have spent a negative amount of time inside. Researchers tested whether this was merely a misleading artifact by making extremely weak measurements of the atoms. Surprisingly, the atoms confirmed the same negative dwell time, adding a new puzzle to quantum physics.

By eliminating heavy compression hardware, the design could shrink power plants and improve efficiency, unlocking more effective clean-energy and future-aviation systems. The approach may also reduce emissions in power generation.

The Scientific Research subsection presents advances in biomedicine, paleontology, and molecular archaeology. A chewing gum reduces oral HPV by up to 93%, a 518-million-year-old fossil reveals the origin of spider fangs, underwater cave bones offer new clues for reconstructing ancient megafauna remains, and DNA analysis of the Shroud of Turin uncovers centuries of biological imprints from human handling and travel.

Cancer-fighting chewing gum cuts HPV levels by up to 93% ScienceDaily 科学

A specially engineered chewing gum reduced HPV levels by up to 93% and nearly eliminated two bacteria linked to head and neck cancer, while preserving beneficial oral microbes. The findings raise hopes for a safer, cheaper therapy that could be integrated into daily oral care.

Read more → 518-million-year-old creature reveals the origins of spider fangs ScienceDaily 科学

A tiny 518-million-year-old sea creature has yielded the earliest known evidence of structures that eventually became spiders' fangs. Hidden inside the fossil were pincer-like limbs and preserved soft tissues, helping explain how ancestors of spiders and scorpions evolved into formidable hunters. The discovery fills a key gap in the evolution of these arachnids.

Read more → These underwater bones could solve a mystery about extinct giants ScienceDaily 科学

Animal bones preserved in South Australia's underwater caves bear distinctive clues shaped by darkness, algae, bacteria, and other environmental conditions. The findings give scientists a powerful new way to reconstruct how ancient megafauna remains entered and survived in submerged cave systems. The work refines methods for studying fossils in extreme environments.

Read more → DNA on the Shroud of Turin reveals centuries of hidden history ScienceDaily 科学

DNA traces in the Shroud of Turin reveal a remarkable mixture of human lineages, microbes, plants, animals, and coral accumulated through centuries of handling and travel. The findings do not prove its age or authenticity, but they expose a hidden biological history preserved in the famous cloth.
