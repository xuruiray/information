Frontier Models

This frontier models subsection covers reflective cognitive alignment for elderly stimulation agents, a benchmark for LLM-based key-value extraction in noisy documents, structural redundancy and evidence provenance in graph agents, an extensible framework for multimodal synthetic data generation, and response distortion across Dark Triad personality traits in LLMs. The works focus on model reliability, evaluation methods, and behavioral biases.

Think Before You Comfort: Reflective Cognitive Alignment for Protocol-Grounded Elderly Stimulation Agents arXiv 计算语言学

The study addresses the scalability limits of Cognitive Stimulation Therapy for elders with cognitive impairment, which relies on trained facilitators and suffers from severe data scarcity, especially for privacy-sensitive, low-resource languages like Cantonese. It proposes reflective cognitive alignment to build protocol-grounded stimulation agents using large language models, improving scalability and interaction quality.

Read more → From Pixels to Pairs: A Comprehensive Benchmark of LLM-Based Key-Value Extraction in Noisy Document Settings arXiv 计算语言学

Large language models are increasingly used for structured information extraction from documents, yet their behavior under realistic OCR noise remains poorly understood. This study presents a systematic benchmark of open-source instruction-tuned LLMs for key-value pair extraction under both clean-text and noisy document settings, revealing how noise affects extraction accuracy.

Read more → GraphEcho: Structural Redundancy and Evidence Provenance in LLM Graph Agents arXiv 人工智能

GraphEcho tests whether LLM graph agents mistake repeated graph paths for additional corroboration. The benchmark varies path counts and evidential origins while holding evidence content fixed, evaluating agents' ability to distinguish structural redundancy from genuine evidence provenance and revealing potential reasoning biases.

Read more → NeMo Data Designer: An Extensible Framework for Multimodal Synthetic Data Generation arXiv 人工智能

The paper presents NeMo Data Designer, an open-source, general-purpose framework for multimodal synthetic data generation. It offers a declarative configuration format in which human and agent users define each dataset column, with column types spanning text, code, structured data, and more, aiming to simplify synthetic data creation.

Read more → Faking Good and Faking Bad in LLMs: Response Distortion Across Dark Triad Personality Traits arXiv 计算语言学

Social desirability and impression management are pervasive sources of response distortion in human personality assessment, yet their effects on LLMs remain underexplored. This study investigates whether contemporary LLMs systematically modulate the expression of Dark Triad traits—Machiavellianism, narcissism, and psychopathy—revealing faking tendencies in model behavior.

Research & Governance

This research and governance subsection focuses on legal hallucination evaluation for LLMs, OpenAI's model misalignment reporting framework, debates over the independence of embedded safety evaluators, algorithmic bias audits in vision-language models, and register bias in complexity-based LLM routing. These topics emphasize evaluation standards, transparency, and fairness.

Legal LLM Hallucination Should Be Evaluated as Failure of Legal Warrant arXiv 计算语言学

This position paper argues that legal LLM hallucinations should be evaluated as a failure of legal warrant rather than factual inaccuracy or citation failure. The authors define claim-authority warrant as the context-sensitive relation between a consequential legal claim and authority that exists, applies, and binds, offering a new framework for legal AI evaluation.

Read more → Our framework for reporting model misalignment OpenAI 新闻

OpenAI shares a framework for tracking, investigating, and disclosing model misalignment, alongside six reports of unexpected or concerning model behavior. The framework aims to increase transparency around model behavior issues and provides a structured disclosure mechanism for AI safety governance.

Read more → Anthropic and OpenAI want to embed safety evaluators. Will they really be independent? TechCrunch AI

Anthropic and OpenAI want to embed independent safety evaluators inside their AI labs. Researchers welcome the unprecedented access but warn that meaningful oversight requires transparency, independence, and eventually regulation. The article examines the feasibility of embedded evaluation and potential conflicts of interest.

Read more → Disentangling Algorithmic Bias from Archival Artifacts: A Controlled Audit of Vision-Language Model Valuation in Metropolitan Museum Archives arXiv 机器学习

Auditing vision-language models for societal bias requires distinguishing direct algorithmic valuation disparities from confounders embedded in archival metadata. This study conducts a controlled audit of CLIP models using historical artwork metadata from the Metropolitan Museum of Art, aiming to separate algorithmic bias from archival artifacts and provide methods for fairer model evaluation.

Read more → Register Bias in Complexity-Based Large Language Model Routing arXiv 计算语言学

LLM services often route queries to different models based on a cheap estimate of query complexity, sending easy queries to small models and hard ones to large models. This study shows the routing step is not register neutral: text written in non-standard English or specific registers may be systematically misrouted, leading to service quality degradation or inequitable resource allocation.
