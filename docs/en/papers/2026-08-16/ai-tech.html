Agreement Is Not Alignment: Divergent Moral Grounds in Human and LLM Ethical Judgments arXiv 人工智能

A new paper argues that agreement on final labels is a flawed proxy for alignment. Humans and LLMs may reach the same ethical judgment while relying on different moral grounds, so evaluating the reasoning process is essential to measure true alignment. The study calls for richer evaluations that look beyond aggregate agreement.

Read more → Diagnostic Foundation for Evaluating LLMs' Research Integrity as Co-Scientists arXiv 人工智能

The paper introduces IntegrityBench, a benchmark that measures LLMs’ ability to classify misconduct, reason about ethical actions, and make evidence-grounded decisions under institutional pressure across 36 paired scenarios. It provides a diagnostic foundation for deploying LLMs as co-scientists while safeguarding research norms. The benchmark targets real-world tensions between productivity and integrity.

This work proposes MAS-DecStream, an LLM-assisted contract net negotiation framework for decentralized stream-processing scheduling across heterogeneous mobile edge–cloud systems, addressing workload volatility and QoS constraints. The approach aims to improve resource utilization and service guarantees in distributed environments, representing a step toward autonomous LLM-driven system management.

Read more → Large Language Models Can Follow Instructions, But Not Many at Once: Phase Transitions in Compositional Constraint Satisfaction arXiv 人工智能

The study finds that LLMs handle individual constraints well but show phase-transition-like degradation when reasoning structure, safety boundaries, and output schemas must be satisfied jointly, highlighting limits in compositional instruction following. This has direct implications for evaluating model capabilities and designing reliable multi-constraint deployments. The authors call for new benchmarks that focus on compositional regimes.

AI Products & Platforms

This subsection highlights a study showing that the language of a prompt can change an LLM's decisions in high-stakes scenarios such as nuclear deterrence. Testing nine models from six providers, it calls for broader multilingual safety evaluation in AI products. The findings suggest that English-only alignment testing may be insufficient, and product teams should consider language-specific risk assessment. This has direct implications for international deployment and safety governance.

Don't Want Your LLM to Recommend Nuclear Strike? Try Asking It in Japanese arXiv 人工智能

Testing nine models from six providers, the study finds that changing the prompt language can alter an LLM’s decision in a high-stakes strategic scenario, yet safety alignment is typically evaluated only in English. The results raise concerns about the robustness of alignment across languages and cultures, which may be critical for international users of AI-based advisory systems.

Research & Governance

A position paper argues that AI systems used in high-stakes decisions should reason in ways cognitively aligned with their users and faithfully communicate that reasoning. It highlights that current alignment often optimizes outcomes without ensuring the model's rationale matches the user's mental model, and proposes a practical agenda for designing, measuring, and deploying cognitively aligned systems.

Position: We Need Practical AI Alignment Methods to Mirror Human Reasoning arXiv 人工智能

As AI becomes a decision aid, delegate, or autonomous decision-maker, this position paper argues that high-stakes systems should reason similarly to their users and faithfully communicate that reasoning. It highlights that current alignment often optimizes outcomes without ensuring the model's rationale matches the user's mental model, and proposes a practical agenda for designing, measuring, and deploying cognitively aligned systems.
