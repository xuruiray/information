Science and Technology: Thermal Cloaking, AI Early Detection and an Ancient Eclipse

This edition covers a 3D thermal invisibility cloak that guides heat around objects, making them disappear from infrared imaging in nearly any direction while shielding them from extreme temperatures; research showing that regular exercise reshapes the nerves controlling heart function, with different changes on the left and right sides of the body; an AI system called REDMOD that identified hidden pancreatic cancer signs an average of 475 days before diagnosis and was nearly twice as sensitive as experienced radiologists; a reconstruction of a 709 BCE total solar eclipse after correcting an 8-kilometer error in the location of an ancient Chinese capital, yielding more accurate measurements of Earth's rotation and ancient solar activity; and the discovery in China of a giant fungus dubbed D-rex, whose lineage appears to have emerged shortly after the dinosaurs went extinct.

Research: Heart Nerve Rewiring, AI Pancreatic Cancer Detection and Ancient Eclipse Clues

New research shows that regular exercise not only strengthens the heart but also reshapes the nerves controlling heart function, with notably different changes on the left and right sides of the body. An AI system called REDMOD identified hidden pancreatic cancer signs an average of 475 days before diagnosis and was nearly twice as sensitive as experienced radiologists. By correcting an 8-kilometer error in the location of an ancient Chinese capital, scientists reconstructed a total solar eclipse from 709 BCE and obtained more accurate measurements of Earth's rotation and ancient solar activity. Chinese scientists also discovered a giant fungus dubbed D-rex, growing up to 20 inches tall and weighing nearly five pounds, with a lineage that appears to have emerged shortly after the dinosaurs went extinct.
