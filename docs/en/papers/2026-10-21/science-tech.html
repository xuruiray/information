Science and Technology: Deep-Space Nuclear Power, Early-Universe Helium and Biomedical Findings

This Science and Technology briefing covers NASA’s progress on the SR-1 Freedom nuclear power module, intended to demonstrate deep-space nuclear electric propulsion and, if successful, become the first fission-powered spacecraft operating beyond Earth orbit; an international team’s unprecedented-precision measurement of helium formed in the first five minutes after the Big Bang using 15 pristine galaxies. In research, imaging shows statins can bind to and force open a key muscle-cell protein, explaining muscle pain in some patients; improved hurricane forecast graphics do not necessarily raise perceived risk or protective action; a rare barrel-shaped belemnite, the ‘fattest’ ever found, was identified in roughly 160-million-year-old Wyoming deposits; and blocking immune cells involved in neuron destruction reduced Alzheimer’s-related brain damage and improved memory in mice without removing toxic tau.

Statin Muscle Pain, Hurricane Warnings, Jurassic Fossil and Alzheimer’s Research

Advanced imaging shows statins can attach to a crucial muscle-cell protein and force it open, explaining why some patients develop muscle pain, weakness or severe damage. A new study finds improved hurricane forecast graphics help identify threatened areas but do not necessarily increase perceived danger or protective action, and misunderstanding of the hurricane cone persists. In Wyoming, researchers identified the ‘fattest’ belemnite ever found—a barrel-shaped squid relative from about 160 million years ago—revealing a previously unknown form and a possible small cephalopod predator. In a mouse study, blocking immune cells involved in neuron destruction reduced Alzheimer’s-related brain damage; memory centers retained about 40% more tissue and memory improved even without removing toxic tau.
