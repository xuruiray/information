AI & LLMs: Frontier Models, Products, and Governance

This edition covers frontier models, AI products and platforms, and research and governance. On frontier models, the papers discuss adaptive personalization for conversational AI under preference uncertainty, AutoHebb self-modifying neural networks combining zero-shot inference and continual learning, and catastrophic forgetting in a physics foundation model during sim-to-real fine-tuning. On products, datasette-llm released version 0.1a9 with automatic use of LLM’s default model, while privacy-focused AI assistant Ollie will shut down in a week. On research and governance, items include CoFiBreak’s diversity reward for multi-turn jailbreaks, pruning redundant attention heads to suppress verbatim memorization, the Qistas fairness evaluation protocol, the BILAL-BENCH benchmark for Islamic-source fabrication in Bengali, and a judge allowing the NAACP lawsuit over xAI data center pollution to proceed.

Frontier Models: Personalization, Self-Modification, and Sim-to-Real Forgetting

Three papers respectively propose adaptive personalization under preference epistemic uncertainty, introduce AutoHebb self-modifying neural networks for zero-shot inference and continual learning, and examine catastrophic forgetting in a physics foundation model under sim-to-real fine-tuning.

Adaptive Personalization Under Preference Epistemic Uncertainty arXiv 计算语言学

The paper proposes an adaptive personalization approach for conversational AI that adjusts response strategies as user preferences remain stable, become uncertain, or are explicitly revised, rather than relying only on passive memory retrieval.

Read more → AutoHebb: Self-Modifying Neural Networks that Zero-Shot Infer and Continually Learn arXiv 机器学习

The paper introduces AutoHebb, a self-modifying neural network aimed at combining zero-shot inference and continual learning, while noting limits in existing models that simulate these abilities.

Read more → Frozen in Time: How a Physics Foundation Model Forgets Under Sim-to-Real Adaptation arXiv 机器学习

The study examines catastrophic forgetting in scientific foundation models during sim-to-real fine-tuning, noting that while such models adapt to unseen data distributions, additional fine-tuning can degrade prior capabilities.

Research and Governance: Jailbreaks, Memorization Suppression, Fairness Evaluation, and Legal Developments

Research items include CoFiBreak’s coarse-to-fine diversity reward, pruning redundant attention heads to suppress verbatim memorization, the pre-registered and portable Qistas fairness evaluation protocol, and BILAL-BENCH for benchmarking Islamic-source fabrication in Bengali. On governance, a judge allowed the NAACP lawsuit over xAI data center pollution to proceed.

CoFiBreak: A Coarse-to-Fine Diversity Reward for Effective Multi-Turn Jailbreak arXiv 计算语言学

The paper introduces CoFiBreak, a coarse-to-fine diversity reward for training attacker LLMs in multi-turn jailbreaks, aiming to reduce prompt-diversity collapse when attack success is the only reward and to improve safety evaluation.

Read more → Fewer Heads, Fewer Memories: Pruning Redundant Attention Heads Suppresses Verbatim Memorisation arXiv 计算语言学

The study examines pruning redundant attention heads to suppress verbatim memorization in large language models, aiming to reduce privacy leakage and intellectual-property exposure while considering effects on generalization.

Read more → Qistas: A Pre-Registered, Portable Protocol for Fairness Evaluation of Large Language Models arXiv 计算语言学

The paper presents Qistas, a pre-registered and portable protocol for evaluating LLM fairness, addressing benchmarks that are largely English-born and American in their social categories and often do not fit other societies after translation.

Read more → Do Open Language Models Invent Scripture? BILAL-BENCH: Benchmarking Islamic-Source Fabrication in Bengali arXiv 计算语言学

The paper introduces BILAL-BENCH to benchmark fabrication of Qur’anic verses and hadith by open language models in Bengali, noting that existing Islamic hallucination benchmarks are largely Arabic and often closed-ended.
