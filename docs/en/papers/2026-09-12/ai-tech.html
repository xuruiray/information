Frontier Models Briefing

This subsection covers model training, inference scheduling, and generalization. Nemotron uses supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning for olympiad proof generation; research proposes tail-aware scheduling for agentic workflows and task-agnostic environment preprocessing. It also includes a quantitative study of grokking and an Anthropic researcher's public warning about the race to superintelligence.

Decoupling Readiness from Release for Tail-Aware Scheduling of Agentic LLM Workflows arXiv 人工智能

The paper studies end-to-end completion time of agentic LLM workflows, noting it depends not only on inference speed but also on when ready turns are released. It proposes decoupling readiness from release to improve scheduling under contention, addressing the limitations of eager release policies in agentic systems.

Read more → An Open Recipe for IMO Gold: Training Nemotron for Olympiad Mathematics arXiv 人工智能

The paper examines how post-training and test-time inference design affect natural-language proof generation for hard olympiad mathematics. Starting from Nemotron 3 Ultra, it trains two specialist checkpoints via supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning, and evaluates checkpoint choice and verification. The work provides an open recipe for olympiad-level reasoning.

Read more → Studying Without a Syllabus: Task-Agnostic Environment Preprocessing arXiv 人工智能

The paper explores how LLM agents can inspect available corpora and tools to build reusable resources like indices, scripts, or procedural guidance before tackling tasks in new environments. Unlike most automated adaptation methods that rely on task examples or evaluation feedback, it proposes task-agnostic preprocessing.

An Anthropic researcher resigned this week and warned in a post on X that the company is “racing straight to self-improving superintelligence and gambling with our lives.” The company’s own alignment lead co-signed the message rather than walking it back. Read more → Quantifying the Memorization-to-Generalization Transition: Scaling Laws and Phase Structure in Grokking arXiv 人工智能

The paper studies grokking, the delayed transition to generalization after memorization in neural networks. It maps the quantitative structure of when this transition occurs in hyperparameter space, characterizing scaling laws and phase structure, complementing prior theoretical work on why it occurs. The findings offer theoretical insights for training large models.

Research & Governance Briefing

This subsection covers search algorithms and cognitive reasoning frameworks. One paper proposes probabilistic focal search to accelerate bounded-suboptimal search via lower-bound advancement; another presents a multi-stage rule-chaining framework for compositional reasoning across symbolic, structural, and conceptual levels, evaluated on the ARC benchmark. Both aim to improve interpretability and efficiency in AI reasoning.

Probabilistic Focal Search: Accelerating Bounded-Suboptimal Search via Lower-Bound Advancement arXiv 人工智能

The paper introduces probabilistic focal search for bounded-suboptimal search. Traditional focal search uses heuristic guidance within a threshold, but its deterministic policy may leave the minimum f-value unchanged for many expansions. The new method accelerates search via lower-bound advancement. It reduces the number of expansions required.

Read more → A Multi-Stage Rule-Chaining Framework for Compositional and Interpretable Cognitive Reasoning arXiv 人工智能

The paper presents a multi-stage rule-chaining framework that performs compositional reasoning across symbolic, structural, and conceptual levels. It evaluates cognitive generalization on the Abstraction and Reasoning Corpus (ARC), the ability to infer and apply abstract rules from limited examples. The work aims to improve interpretability in AI reasoning.
