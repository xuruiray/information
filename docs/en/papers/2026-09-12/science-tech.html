Scientific Research Briefing

This Scientific Research briefing spans physics, medicine and planetary science. Physicists investigate a tiny fundamental uncertainty in time itself; researchers show for the first time that human heart muscle can regrow after a heart attack; new simulations suggest the Moon may have formed intact within hours of a giant impact; and cord blood stem cells are engineered into cancer-fighting T cells.

Physicists just found a tiny glitch in time itself ScienceDaily 科学

Unconventional quantum theories may imply that time itself has a tiny fundamental uncertainty, placing an ultimate limit on how precisely any clock could measure it. The effect is far beyond current detection, but it could reveal a hidden connection between quantum mechanics, gravity and spacetime.

Read more → Scientists discover the human heart can regrow muscle after a heart attack ScienceDaily 科学

Researchers have shown for the first time that human heart muscle cells can regrow after a heart attack. The finding overturns the idea that damaged areas of the heart are completely incapable of regeneration. Scientists now hope to identify ways to dramatically boost this natural repair process, potentially opening a new path toward treatments for heart failure.

Read more → The Moon may have formed intact just hours after a giant collision ScienceDaily 科学

Scientists have discovered that the Moon-forming collision may have depended heavily on how hot and physically strong early Earth and Theia were. New simulations found that some impacts create the familiar disk of debris that later becomes the Moon, while others can produce an intact Moon in only about five hours. The results challenge a long-standing assumption.
