2026 in LLMs (so far)

Simon Willison's closing keynote at WeAreDevelopers World Congress North America ties together the key LLM trends of the past year in a chronological exploration of 2026. The post includes the video, annotated slides and notes, making it a useful recap of model, agent and developer-tool shifts so far. It is a structured review from a widely read developer commentator rather than a breaking-news report.

Simon Willison
BioEVAL: A global, multi-institutional benchmark of large language and multimodal models for bioengineering A multi-institutional team introduces BioEVAL, a benchmark of large language and multimodal models for bioengineering tasks. It targets frontier and multimodal capabilities rather than factual recall, offering a standardised way to compare models on scientific problem solving across research groups. The authors argue existing benchmarks under-represent such frontier work. arXiv 人工智能 ScopeBench: Do Agents Preserve Engagement Boundaries Under Goal Pressure? ScopeBench evaluates autonomous agents on whether they stay within a client's authorized engagement scope during web and network penetration testing. With offensive-security benchmarks saturating, the authors argue that boundary compliance, not raw hacking skill, is now the main barrier to deployment. The benchmark is designed for real autonomous deployments. arXiv 人工智能 Not All Memories Are Equal: Hierarchical Collaborative Memory for Validity-Aware Retrieval in LLM Agents The paper proposes a hierarchical collaborative memory scheme that separates team-level decisions and consensus from individual observations and execution traces, then performs validity-aware retrieval. The goal is to improve how LLM agents recall and use information in team settings. It treats memory as heterogeneous and evolving rather than a single store. arXiv 计算语言学
