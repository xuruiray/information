China Watch: Plenum Decision Details Anti-Corruption and Party Rule, Alongside Industry and Legal Developments

On Oct. 30, a Communist Party briefing on the 20th Central Committee’s fifth plenum detailed the Party decision’s arrangements on anti-corruption, democratic centralism, cadre standards and rule enforcement. The same day, China’s first Yangtze Delta joint ship-design review center opened in Shanghai and a trusted data space for the meteorological industry was launched; the Ministry of Public Security sought comments on revised administrative case procedures, the China Consumers Association warned that “air disinfection cards” involve false advertising, and multiple cities began removing an invasive species.

Party Briefing on Decision Deploys Anti-Corruption, Democratic Centralism and Rule Enforcement

At an Oct. 30 briefing on the 20th Central Committee’s fifth plenum, CCDI Deputy Secretary Zhang Fuhai said the Party decision sets out a four-pronged anti-corruption path with Chinese characteristics and cited 1.644 million “four malfeasances” cases since the 18th Party Congress. Central Propaganda Department Deputy Head Hu Heping said the decision makes strict democratic centralism a key focus of full and rigorous Party governance with four arrangements. Central Organization Department Deputy Head Huang Jianfa said it stresses the good-cadre standard and calls for removing cadres who are disloyal, duplicitous or use power for private gain. Xie Chuntao of the Central Party School said the decision’s eighth section specifically requires stronger enforcement and rigid constraints in implementing Party rules.
