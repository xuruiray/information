Scientific Research: Higher-Energy X-Rays, Sweeteners and Brain-Health Links

By making two electrons work together, researchers generated X-rays with much higher energies than conventional theory predicts, defying a long-standing laser-physics limit; a study of nearly 13,000 adults linked six common sweeteners to faster declines in memory and thinking; in more than 87,000 adults, greater daytime light exposure was associated with lower dementia risk, while less than 42 minutes of bright daylight was tied to higher risk; and scientists found a weakened brain signal that may link sensory processing difficulties, disorganized speech and greater symptom severity in schizophrenia.

Scientists just broke a textbook rule of physics ScienceDaily 科学

Scientists have generated X-rays with much higher energies than conventional theory predicts by making two electrons work together, defying a long-standing laser-physics limit and opening possibilities for quantum research.

Read more → Six popular sugar substitutes linked to faster brain aging ScienceDaily 科学

A study of nearly 13,000 adults found those consuming the most artificial sweeteners had a 62% faster decline in memory and thinking, equal to about 1.6 extra years of brain aging; six sweeteners were linked to faster decline.

In more than 87,000 adults, greater daytime light exposure was associated with a 16% lower dementia risk, with longer bright-light exposure linked to greater reductions; less than 42 minutes of bright daylight was tied to higher risk.
