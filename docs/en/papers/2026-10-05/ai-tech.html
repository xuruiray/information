Frontier Models Briefing

The Frontier Models subsection highlights agent value estimation for long-horizon tool use, terminal-agent training and verification, and new benchmarks for closed-loop evaluation in Chinese chess and multi-turn user simulation.

Choosing Before Acting: Comparative Value Estimation for Long-Horizon Tool-Use Agents arXiv 人工智能

The paper introduces a comparative value estimation method for long-horizon tool-use agents, using step-level rewards to address weak credit assignment in long interaction traces and improve tool invocation decisions in complex tasks.

Read more → THPL: A Vision-to-Language Decision Support Framework for Rainbow Trout Feeding Management in RAS arXiv 人工智能

The paper presents THPL, a vision-to-language decision support framework that translates fish behavior into interpretable feeding decisions for precision feeding in recirculating aquaculture systems, aiming to cut costs and improve fish welfare.

Read more → When Terminal-Agent Training Stalls: Demystifying Data Generation and Verification Challenge arXiv 人工智能

The paper examines the fidelity of end-to-end pipelines when using frontier models as meta-agents to generate terminal tasks and verifiers, noting that runnable Docker images and executable test suites do not guarantee effective training.

Read more → Finding the Move Is Not Winning the Game: XiangqiBench for Closed-Loop Evaluation of LLM Agents arXiv 计算语言学

The paper introduces XiangqiBench, an executable Chinese chess benchmark starting from 119 tactical endgames, measuring whether LLM agents can carry a plan to a verified outcome against an opponent, not just name the right move.

Read more → CUEing User Simulators: Calibrated User Embeddings for Multi-Turn Benchmarking arXiv 计算语言学

The paper proposes calibrated user embeddings to improve the ecological validity of user simulators in multi-turn benchmarking, aligning when and how agents fail across simulated and real user interactions.

Research & Governance Briefing

The Research & Governance subsection covers AI safety culture, state-space unlearning for non-stationary bias, multi-agent conformity with preserved premises, and generalization evaluation of LLMs in temporal extraction.

OpenAI safety employee resigns, claiming the company’s ‘culture is broken’ TechCrunch AI

OpenAI safety team member David Robinson resigns, warning that the company's safety culture is broken. He acknowledges being a cliché but says his warning deserves attention.

Read more → State-Space Unlearning for Non-Stationary Bias in Land Surface Forecasting arXiv 机器学习

The paper proposes a state-space unlearning method for land surface forecasting systems built on Mamba-family models, removing hidden biases from unrecorded events like irrigation or dam operations that affect NDVI and LST predictions.

Read more → Silent Dissent: LLM Agents That Yield to the Majority Still Represent Their Original Premise arXiv 计算语言学

The paper studies whether LLM agents that change answers under unanimous majority pressure truly change their minds. Using two-hop factual questions, it finds that agents may yield in statement but still represent their original premise internally.

Read more → Evaluating Multi-Dimensional Generalization of Large Language Models in Temporal Extraction Tasks arXiv 计算语言学

The paper evaluates multi-dimensional generalization of LLMs in temporal extraction tasks under distribution shifts, noting that annotation ambiguity and domain sensitivity cause unstable model behavior, while existing evaluations offer limited insight into cross-domain reliability.
