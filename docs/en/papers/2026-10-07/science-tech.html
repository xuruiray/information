Animals may have evolved 200 million years earlier than we thought ScienceDaily 科学

New research suggests animals could have evolved as early as 800 to 700 million years ago, roughly 200 million years earlier than some previous estimates. If so, the first animals may have lived before or during Snowball Earth, leaving little trace because their soft bodies were rarely preserved.

Read more → Goodbye joint replacements? Stanford scientists found a way to regrow cartilage and stop arthritis ScienceDaily 科学

Scientists have found a surprising way to regrow damaged knee cartilage by blocking a protein that becomes more abundant with age. In old mice, the treatment restored naturally lost cartilage and also helped prevent osteoarthritis after ACL-like knee injuries, while human cartilage taken from knee replacement patients began producing new, functional joint cartilage.

Read more → What happens in the brain when cannabis makes you anxious? ScienceDaily 科学

Cannabinoids may intensify anxiety by removing a natural brake on specific neurons in the brain's fear center, particularly during stressful situations. In mice, shutting down those neurons sharply reduced cannabinoid-related anxious behavior.

Read more → Humans may not be as hardwired for violence as we thought ScienceDaily 科学

A study comparing 100 primate species, including humans, suggests that everyday aggression and lethal violence did not evolve as part of the same simple behavioral spectrum. Species that frequently fight or show mild aggression are not necessarily more likely to kill rivals, while extreme acts such as infanticide and lethal attacks appear to follow their own path.
