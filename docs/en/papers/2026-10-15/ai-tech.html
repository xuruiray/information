This edition covers new papers and product moves in AI and large models. On frontier models, ClawLoop and Harness^2 explore self-improvement for LLM agents through a harness-native training loop and recursive execution harnessing, Echo uses error-compensated 4-bit tensor-parallel communication to cut all-reduce costs, and another paper asks whether LLMs can access world knowledge for event prediction. Frontier Models

4 articles
Frontier Models: Self-Improving Agents, Communication Optimization, and Event Prediction

The frontier models subsection covers four arXiv papers. ClawLoop lets LLM-based agents self-improve through a harness-native training loop in sandbox workspaces, improving their use of tools such as file systems and shells. Harness^2 proposes recursive self-improvement to automatically evolve execution harnesses for LLM agents and discusses two challenges in open-world settings. Echo uses error-compensated 4-bit tensor-parallel communication to cut all-reduce costs in LLM training and inference and ease interconnect bandwidth bottlenecks. Another paper asks whether LLMs truly cannot access pretrained world knowledge to predict events, or whether current methods fail to present the event prediction task effectively.

Research & Governance

5 articles
Research and Governance: Superintelligence Tests, Long-Horizon Benchmarks, and Pluralistic Alignment

The research and governance subsection summarizes five arXiv papers. One proposes an LLM-referenced C-test to evaluate machines that may surpass current systems and test designers, avoiding benchmarks that become circular or saturate quickly. Long-Horizon-Terminal-Bench 2.0 extends a long-horizon terminal benchmark, retaining 46 original tasks while automating diverse task construction and using process rewards to measure whether agents sustain progress through workflows. Another paper studies welfare-aware preference acquisition for pluralistic alignment under limited human feedback, using AI simulators while accounting for varying simulation accuracy so diverse preferences are better represented in alignment training. A further paper examines evaluation of LLM data science agents from task formulation to model development, arguing that final outcomes alone miss silent failures in the process. The last compares open-weight models with expensive closed frontier models on the CTI-REALM benchmark, studying trade-offs for organizations choosing auditable language-model agents for security work.
