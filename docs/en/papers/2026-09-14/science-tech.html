Space, Energy & Robotics Briefing

This subsection covers three advances in quantum technology and planetary science. Harvard researchers protected a diamond-based qubit with microscopic mechanical vibrations, extending coherence roughly threefold. Another project combines an electron microscope with a quantum computer to extract clearer detail using fewer electrons, helping preserve fragile samples. Analysis of Mercury's surface finds lower-than-expected silicon dioxide, pointing to a hotter, more extensive volcanic past.

Tiny Sound Waves Could Help Solve a Major Quantum Computing Problem ScienceDaily 技术

Harvard researchers demonstrated a way to protect quantum information using microscopic sound waves. By continuously surrounding a diamond-based qubit with mechanical vibrations, they extended its coherence time roughly threefold. The same phonons could eventually both transmit and protect quantum information, pointing toward compact sound-based quantum devices.

Scientists are combining an electron microscope with a quantum computer to extract far more information from each electron. The approach could reveal faint details with fewer electrons, helping protect fragile samples that conventional microscopy can damage, and highlights quantum computing's potential in precision imaging.

Read more → Mercury's Surface Reveals a Surprisingly Fiery Past ScienceDaily 技术

Scientists found that Mercury's surface contains far less silicon dioxide than previously thought, pointing to an unexpectedly hot volcanic past. The result suggests ancient lava may have originated from deeper, more extensively melted parts of the planet's mantle, offering clues to Mercury's interior evolution.

NASA-Backed Scientists Turn Plastic Waste into Edible Cookies ScienceDaily 科学

Scientists engineered yeast to turn PET plastic and agricultural waste into proteins, fats, vitamins and even vanilla flavoring, producing protein-rich 3D-printed cookies called µBites. Developed partly for NASA's Deep Space Food Challenge, the system is aimed at food production in space and other extreme environments.
