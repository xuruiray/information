Frontier Models

5 articles
Frontier Models

The Frontier Models subsection focuses on agentic alignment, long-horizon strategic reasoning, and self-evolution. CLEAT studies value-guided clarification for interactive agents; SpireBench evaluates long-horizon strategic play using Slay the Spire; EvoBelief evolves agents' beliefs about environment dynamics. Two additional benchmarks target grounded question answering over geopolitical intelligence reports and elicitation of geospatial data requirements. Together they expand evaluation of reasoning, planning, and knowledge grounding in large models.

CLEAT: Learning Value-Guided Clarification for Interactive Agents arXiv 人工智能

The paper studies interactive intent alignment through clarification, where agents must act under goals and constraints users may leave unstated. It examines when clarification is useful and how an agent can turn answers into improved actions, framing clarification as value-guided learning for interactive agents.

Read more → SpireBench: Evaluating Long-Horizon Strategic Play of Agentic Models with Slay the Spire arXiv 人工智能

Most agentic benchmarks are short and episodic, leaving long-horizon strategic reasoning under-studied. SpireBench has models play the full game Slay the Spire, aiming to dissect performance into stored knowledge, reasoning, and luck over extended tasks.

Read more → EvoBelief: Falsifiable World Models for Self-Improving LLM Agents arXiv 人工智能

Self-evolving agents often improve acting through experience, reflection, or skills, yet can fail when their beliefs about environment dynamics are incomplete or outdated. EvoBelief studies evolving those beliefs as a complementary axis of self-evolution and emphasizes falsifiability.

Read more → GeopoliticalQA: Benchmarking Retrieval and Generation over Intelligence Reports arXiv 计算语言学

Geopolitical and strategic intelligence reports hold expert knowledge but are heterogeneous and analytically complex, posing challenges for retrieval-augmented question answering. The paper introduces a benchmark for grounded question answering over such reports, evaluating retrieval and generation capabilities.

Read more → GeoFitBench-LLM: A Benchmark for Assessing Large Language Models' Capabilities in Geospatial Data Requirements Elicitation for Geo-Analytic Tasks arXiv 计算语言学

Large language models are becoming central to GeoAI systems, yet evaluation rarely considers elicitation of geospatial data requirements. The paper argues that retrieval-oriented geo-analytic tasks cannot be reliably supported without first identifying necessary datasets and constraints, and introduces a benchmark for this capability.

Research & Governance

Research & Governance

The Research & Governance subsection covers multimodal reward modeling, agent guardrails, and explainability. Work on unsupervised multimodal reward modeling seeks to avoid human preference labels. VEPA offers verified, evolving formal policy guardrails for long-running agents. A position paper argues that explainable AI faithfulness metrics suffer from structural misalignments.

Unsupervised Multimodal Reward Modeling arXiv 计算语言学

Vision-language reward models are typically trained on annotated preference pairs, which are expensive and can disagree. The paper builds a reward model with no human preference labels: a linear head on an intermediate layer of a frozen Qwen3-VL backbone, trained to score a web caption above a corresponding passage.

Read more → Behind-the-Meter Photovoltaic Visibility from Net Smart Meters: Deployment Regimes and Operational Insights arXiv 机器学习

Distribution operators need visibility of behind-the-meter photovoltaic generation, yet most residential customers are observed only through net smart meters. Deep-learning-based PV disaggregation can restore this visibility, but its operational value depends on deployment conditions such as whether the model serves known or new customers.

Read more → MagicBeanStalk: Escaping the RLVR Zero Advantage Trap with Recursive Data Synthesis arXiv 人工智能

Data-centric scaling of RLVR has focused on collecting or generating verifiable training problems, but corpus size alone does not determine whether a dataset provides a useful learning signal. The paper experimentally characterizes dataset effects and guides synthesis of effective RLVR data to escape the zero-advantage trap.

Read more → VEPA: Verified Evolving Formal Policy for Lifelong LLM Agent Guardrails arXiv 人工智能

LLM agents increasingly operate over sensitive resources such as private documents and mutable system states, creating safety risks. Runtime guardrails should make inspectable, reproducible, and deterministic allow-or-refuse decisions before irreversible actions. VEPA proposes verified evolving formal policy to achieve this goal.

Read more → Faithfulness Without Anchors: A Position Paper on the Structural Misalignments of Explainable AI Faithfulness Metrics for Feature Importance arXiv 人工智能

Explainable AI tools have often been presented as ways to increase trust by making models more understandable, but evidence accumulated over the past decade points to an overall failure of this objective. The position paper discusses structural misalignments in explainable AI faithfulness metrics for feature importance.
