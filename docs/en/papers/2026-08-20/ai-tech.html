Frontier Models

Frontier-model research today revolves around reliability and safety. GxP-Agent applies process topology to make agents dependable in clinical trial programming, DiSCO builds adversarial safety for text-to-image generation, and other studies explore uncertainty-aware multimodal reasoning, institution-specific PHI recovery, and cross-model memory transfer, showing the potential of large models in specialized domains.

GxP-Agent: Process-DAG Topology for Reliable Clinical Trial Programming with LLM Agents arXiv 人工智能

Clinical trial programming transforms study protocols into CDISC-compliant analysis-ready datasets, a critical bottleneck in regulatory submissions. Across eleven single-shot attempts with five frontier models, none produces a valid subject-level dataset. GxP-Agent addresses this by introducing a process-DAG topology that guides LLM agents through the required workflow, markedly improving reliability and output validity in a high-stakes medical software setting.

Read more → DiSCO: Defending text-to-image generation through distribution-guided contrastive prompt optimization arXiv 人工智能

Progress in text-to-image generation raises serious safety concerns, especially the production of NSFW content such as violence and nudity, which red-team adversarial attacks can further amplify. Many existing defenses rely on white-box assumptions and access to model internals. DiSCO offers a distribution-guided contrastive prompt optimization method that strengthens the robustness of text-to-image systems against adversarial prompts while preserving generation quality, contributing a practical defense for safer generative AI.

Read more → Institution-Specific LLM Prompting Recovers PHI That De-identification Systems and Their Gold Standards Both Miss arXiv 计算语言学

Secondary use of electronic health records requires robust de-identification, yet standard systems often miss institutionally situated protected health information, such as hospital abbreviations, building names, and internal codes whose sensitivity is locally determined. This study demonstrates that institution-specific LLM prompting can recover PHI overlooked by both contemporary de-identification systems and their gold-standard test sets. The findings suggest a complementary role for large language models in privacy-preserving health-data pipelines, with direct implications for compliance and data governance.

Read more → Uncertainty-Aware Decision Making in Multimodal Large Language Models arXiv 计算语言学

Multimodal large language models increasingly answer questions that depend on visual, textual, temporal, acoustic, document, chart, or embodied evidence, but their failures can stem from poor input quality, perceptual errors, or weak grounding. Since a fluent answer may conceal these problems, understanding and quantifying uncertainty is essential. This paper explores uncertainty-aware decision making in multimodal LLMs, aiming to detect unreliable evidence and improve answer reliability, interpretability, and trustworthy deployment in real-world assistants.

Read more → Cross-Model Memory Transfer via Target-Side Reader Adaptation arXiv 计算语言学

Methods for improving knowledge use in large language models generally separate into non-parametric retrieval, which adds latency and context overhead, and parametric adaptation, which is efficient at inference but costly to retrain. This work proposes cross-model memory transfer via target-side reader adaptation, allowing knowledge from one model to be read by another with better efficiency and deeper integration. The approach seeks to overcome trade-offs between retrieval flexibility and parametric efficiency, offering a promising direction for model reuse and continual knowledge updating.

Research & Governance

On research and governance, OpenAI reaffirmed zero data retention for frontier models and previewed Private Safety Processing. Academics proposed a runtime governance framework for agentic AI, using action boundaries and fail-closed execution to constrain agent behavior and prevent harmful side effects.

Runtime Governance for Agentic AI: Action-Boundary Control with Trusted Provenance and Fail-Closed Execution arXiv 人工智能

Agentic AI systems routinely take tool actions that can modify files, send messages, launch jobs, or alter workflow state, shifting the safety problem from harmful text to harmful operational side effects. Prompt-level governance can influence behavior but does not create a true execution boundary. This paper introduces runtime governance with action-boundary control, trusted provenance, and fail-closed execution, providing a concrete mechanism to keep autonomous agents within approved operational limits and reduce risks from unintended side effects.

It also signals intensified competition among major AI providers over which can offer the strongest data protection and governance guarantees for business clients.
