Policy & Regulation: Flood Response, Ecological Progress and Rule of Law

Coverage this edition focuses on the national flood control headquarters raising its response to Level III for Henan, the Yellow River maintaining continuous flow for 27 years, the Supreme People's Court issuing typical commercial franchise cases, and a local official being placed under investigation. Together they illustrate China's integrated approach to emergency management, ecological preservation, market regulation and anti-corruption.

China Raises Flood Emergency Response to Level III for Henan 中国新闻网时政

On Aug 11, torrential rain or severe downpours hit central-eastern Henan, with extreme rainfall in Pingdingshan and Luoyang. Forecasts say heavy rain will continue from Aug 12 to 15 in parts of the Huang-Huai and southern North China. The national flood control headquarters and the Ministry of Emergency Management raised the response to Level III.

At 8 a.m. on Aug 12, the Lijin hydrological station, the lowest station on the Yellow River, recorded a flow of 2,030 cubic meters per second. Through unified water regulation, the river has maintained continuous flow for 27 years since 1999, strengthening water, food and ecological security.
