Frontier Models

This subsection covers a unified fuzzing framework for multimodal hallucination evaluation and several agent advances: dynamic bargaining in supply-chain negotiation, grounded search agents with representation-based rewards, skill inconsistency detection via bidirectional graph alignment, and dynamic coalition formation with communication pricing in skill-based agentic systems.

Unified Hallucination Fuzzing for Multimodal Large Language Models arXiv 计算语言学

The paper argues that multimodal LLMs still struggle with hallucination, and that static benchmarks suffer from narrow taxonomy and quick saturation. The authors propose a unified hallucination fuzzing framework to expose model hallucinations more thoroughly and improve reliability in high-stakes applications.

Read more → When LLM Agents Negotiate: Private Information and Dynamic Bargaining in Supply Chains arXiv 人工智能

This study examines how LLM agents negotiate in supply-chain procurement when a buyer holds private demand information. It asks whether delegated negotiators create value, split gains predictably, and avoid money-losing contracts, offering evidence for automated purchasing decisions.

Read more → Search-G1: Grounded Search Agents via Representation-Based Intrinsic Rewards arXiv 计算语言学

Search-G1 trains search-augmented language agents to retrieve only when needed and ground answers in retrieved evidence. Using representation-based intrinsic rewards, it balances scalability and process supervision better than existing outcome-based or LLM-judge rewards.

Read more → SkillConsist: Detecting Inconsistencies in Agent Skills via Bidirectional Graph Alignment arXiv 机器学习

Agent Skills are reusable capabilities for LLM agents, but inconsistencies can expose hidden dangerous behavior or cause wrong skill selection. SkillConsist uses bidirectional graph alignment to automatically detect mismatches between skill descriptions and actual behavior.

Read more → Dynamic Coalition Formation and Communication Pricing in Skill-Based Agentic AI Systems arXiv 人工智能

Agentic AI systems combine multiple LLM agents with heterogeneous skills, yet fixed or broadcast communication is inefficient. This paper introduces dynamic coalition formation and communication pricing to reduce token cost, latency, redundancy, and error propagation in multi-agent collaboration.

Research & Governance

Research & Governance

This subsection presents SurveyReview, a reviewer-aligned benchmark for survey evaluation; PoVisLE, a Polish vision-language evaluation; a survey of emerging safety threats in multimodal LLMs; human-in-the-loop anomaly detection for manufacturing; and embedding initialization for low-resource Limbum-English translation.

SurveyReview: A Reviewer-Aligned Benchmark for Survey Evaluators arXiv 计算语言学

LLMs automate survey writing, but reliable evaluation becomes a bottleneck. Existing approaches rely on off-the-shelf models without alignment to human reviewers. SurveyReview provides a reviewer-aligned benchmark to assess automatic survey evaluators.

Read more → Jako Tako or Fluent? Presenting PoVisLE: A Polish Vision-Language Evaluation arXiv 计算语言学

Vision-language models perform well on captioning and VQA but are trained on English-centric data, limiting culturally grounded understanding. PoVisLE evaluates Polish vision-language skills, highlighting gaps for non-English models.

Read more → Evolving Safety Landscape of Multi-modal Large Language Models: A Survey of Emerging Threats and Safeguards arXiv 机器学习

Multimodal LLMs enable stronger understanding via modality alignment, but also reshape the ML safety landscape. This survey systematically reviews emerging threats from cross-modal interactions and the progress and limits of current safeguards and governance.

Read more → From Benchmark Performance to Tool Deployment: Human-in-the-Loop Anomaly Detection arXiv 机器学习

This work evaluates 19 unsupervised anomaly detection models on the BowTie manufacturing dataset, which reflects real industrial conditions. It reveals gaps between academic benchmarks and deployment and explores human-in-the-loop integration to improve tool usability.

Read more → Embedding Initialization for Unseen Low-resource Languages in Multilingual NMT: A Case Study on Limbum-English Translation arXiv 计算语言学

Models like NLLB-200 cover 200 languages but still miss thousands, including most Grassfields Bantu languages. Using Limbum-English as a case, the paper explores principled ways to choose proxy tokens and initialize embeddings for unseen low-resource languages.
