Scientific Research Briefing

This subsection covers four breakthroughs: a vast magma reservoir beneath Tuscany, an AI-assisted counterexample to the Jacobian conjecture, 567-million-year-old fossils reshaping animal origins, and the discovery of brain cells that keep us motivated.

A colossal magma system has been hiding beneath Tuscany ScienceDaily 科学

A massive magma reservoir has been detected deep beneath Tuscany, despite no obvious surface signs. Scientists mapped the roughly 6,000-cubic-kilometer body using natural ground vibrations recorded by dozens of seismic sensors. It poses no immediate danger, but the discovery reveals how enormous volcanic systems can remain hidden and could help scientists better understand volcanic activity.

Read more → Claude Fable 5 AI finds a tiny formula that topples an 87-year-old math conjecture ScienceDaily 科学

A mathematician working at Anthropic says he used the AI model Claude Fable 5 to uncover a remarkably simple counterexample to the Jacobian conjecture, a famous problem that has resisted mathematicians for more than a century. The result shows the conjecture is false in three dimensions and above, although the original two-dimensional version remains unresolved.

Read more → 567-million-year-old fossils rewrite the dawn of animal life ScienceDaily 科学

A spectacular fossil site in Canada contains 567-million-year-old creatures that may represent some of the earliest animals to move, reproduce sexually, and develop recognizable body plans. The discovery pushes key evolutionary milestones back by up to 10 million years and suggests complex animal life may have first flourished in the deep ocean.

Read more → Scientists discover the brain cells that keep you motivated ScienceDaily 科学

A group of brain cells called orexin neurons appears to help us keep working when reaching a reward becomes increasingly difficult. Blocking their activity weakened motivation in rats, pointing to a possible new target for understanding why people sometimes struggle to sustain goal-directed behavior.
