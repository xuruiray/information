Research and Governance: Zero-Shot Medical Decisions, Activation Steering, and Hallucination Detection

The research and governance subsection contains four papers. MedJev evaluates an open System One model that runs locally on a laptop across ten medical benchmarks for zero-shot clinical decision making. Neyman orthogonal activation steering improves estimation of directions that shift LLM outputs toward target behaviors. Low-rank multilinear decomposition is used to detect LLM hallucinations from high-dimensional hidden states. Perturbation-based input ambiguity detection lets models decide whether a prompt admits multiple valid interpretations before answering.
