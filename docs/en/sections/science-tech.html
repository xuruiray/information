Science and Tech: A $10 Sensor Chip, Dark-Energy Evidence and a Precision Brain-Stimulation Therapy

This edition covers three lines of development. Cambridge researchers built a roughly $10 optical sensor small enough for wearables, identifying foods, plastics and medicines with 100% accuracy in tests and non-invasively monitoring blood glucose and alcohol. In space and physics, an analysis of 2,884 exploding stars found evidence that dark energy may change over time, challenging a cornerstone of cosmology, while MIT engineers built a paper-thin swimming robot driven by living muscle cells that respond to light flashes. In research, an FDA-cleared PTSD treatment combining personalized brain mapping with magnetic stimulation reported a 52% symptom reduction and 68% remission in a trial; separate work explained how light-exposed defects in diamond and silicon carbide behave and revealed an underestimated energy-loss mechanism in silicon carbide; a mouse study showed a tiny deep-brain region sends targeted chemical messages rather than broadcasting to the whole brain; and Oxford scientists captured virus-like particles assembling step by step, identifying a molecular turning point that speeds assembly.
