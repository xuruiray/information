Science and Technology Briefing: Telescopes, Quantum States and Brain Research

This Science and Technology briefing covers space observation and home robots as well as fundamental physics, brain aging and depression research. After a tiny mirror adjustment, NASA's Roman Space Telescope delivered image quality nearly 30% better than expected; Webb found three recurring weather states on a planet 20 light-years away; and astronomers reported evidence of a second supermassive black hole at Mrk 501, with the pair potentially colliding within about 100 years. A reporter tested two $55,000 Tau Robotics humanoids, which vacuumed and wiped counters but needed behind-the-scenes human help to load a dishwasher. Fundamental Physics and Brain Health Research

Physicists won a major physics prize for uncovering altermagnetism, a third form of magnetism that challenges more than a century of established physics and could affect future computing, electronics and quantum research. A “fractional Fermi sea” discovered in ultracold atoms is highly excited yet surprisingly orderly, challenging established quantum theories and hinting at exotic quasiparticles. A youth-linked protein protected the brain’s immune cells from age-related decline; restoring it in older mice improved debris clearance and reduced inflammation. Among patients receiving deep brain stimulation for treatment-resistant depression, three brain pathways were linked to lasting improvement, while those who did not improve after two years shared insufficient stimulation of a key connection.
