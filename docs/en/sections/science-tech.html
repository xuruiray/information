New Advances in Space Exploration, Particle Physics and Biomedical Research

This Science & Technology section summarizes eight research developments: Hubble and Webb found 27 ancient objects beyond Neptune; Perseverance reached 28.10 miles of driving on Mars; IceCube detected high-energy neutrinos about 10 billion times the energy of solar neutrinos; and a study suggests a long-overlooked neutrino may explain why matter survived. In biomedical and physics research, a drug originally developed for spinal cord injuries was found to address Alzheimer’s-linked DNA damage and inflammation; the Large Hadron Collider found evidence of quark-gluon plasma in smaller collisions; an experimental drug reversed liver-cell aging in rats and halved an Alzheimer’s-linked protein; and long-term exposure to a common pesticide was linked to more than 2.5 times the risk of Parkinson’s disease.

Objects Beyond Neptune, a Mars Driving Record and High-Energy Neutrinos

Hubble and Webb studied 27 tiny distant objects beyond Neptune, finding they stayed largely unchanged for billions of years and that their unexpected sizes and numbers challenge solar system formation theories. NASA’s Perseverance rover has driven 28.10 miles on Mars, farther than any vehicle on another world. IceCube detected high-energy neutrinos about 10 billion times the energy of solar neutrinos, and another study suggests a long-overlooked neutrino may have reshaped the early universe, explaining why matter survived and possibly leaving detectable gravitational-wave signals.

Advances in Alzheimer’s, Liver-Cell Aging and Parkinson’s Research

A drug originally developed for spinal cord injuries was found to address DNA damage and inflammation linked to Alzheimer’s disease and has undergone initial human safety testing. CERN scientists found that relatively small atomic collisions can recreate quark-gluon plasma, a state hotter than the Sun’s center by more than 100,000 times. USC researchers reported that an experimental drug restored aging liver cells in rats, halved an Alzheimer’s-linked protein and lowered bad cholesterol to youthful levels. A UCLA study linked long-term exposure to a common pesticide with more than 2.5 times the risk of Parkinson’s disease and identified how it damages brain cells and a possible protective approach.
