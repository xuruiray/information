Space, Energy & Robotics Briefing

This subsection covers quantum materials calculation, a laser plasma antenna and an ultracold quantum experiment. Scientists improved Kondo-effect calculations for real materials, demonstrated a tunable laser antenna for radio waves with satellite and radar potential, and observed Bethe strings in ultracold cesium atoms.

A famous quantum effect can finally be predicted in real materials ScienceDaily 技术

Scientists have developed a powerful new way to calculate the Kondo effect, a strange quantum phenomenon that appears when magnetic atoms are embedded in metals. Instead of simplified models, the method uses real atomic and electronic structures, allowing researchers to predict material behavior with far greater accuracy.

Read more → Scientists create a lightsaber-like laser antenna that transmits radio waves ScienceDaily 技术

Scientists have demonstrated a remarkable new antenna resembling a lightsaber, using a laser to create a tunable plasma beam capable of transmitting radio waves. The technology could allow antennas to change size and direction on demand, with promising applications in satellites, radar and space exploration.

Read more → A quantum prediction from 1931 just came to life ScienceDaily 技术

Nearly a century after physicist Hans Bethe predicted them, scientists have created and directly observed unusual quantum states known as “Bethe strings” in an ultracold gas. By cooling cesium atoms near absolute zero and trapping them in thousands of tiny one-dimensional tubes, researchers made groups of atoms bind together without ordinary mechanisms.

Scientific Research Briefing

This subsection focuses on fusion energy routes, planet formation after stellar death, cosmic hydrogen mapping and a crewed space return. Princeton researchers propose heating then increasing density for fusion; Hubble data hints at a possible planet around a white dwarf; CHIME validates a method to map the distant universe; NASA’s Crew-12 completed a 237-day mission.

Scientists may have found a shortcut to fusion energy ScienceDaily 科学

Researchers at Princeton Plasma Physics Laboratory propose a potentially more efficient route to fusion by heating plasma first and then increasing its density. Their calculations suggest future reactors could approach self-sustaining fusion with far less energy while avoiding hidden problems that make ignition harder.

Read more → Hubble Space Telescope may have found a planet born after its star died ScienceDaily 科学

A strange chemical signature in old Hubble observations may point to a planet that formed after its star had already died. Astronomers found unusually high niobium around white dwarf HS 0209+0832, suggesting expelled material may have assembled into a new gas giant. TESS data also hints at a Jupiter-sized planet orbiting nearby.

Read more → A 9-billion-year-old signal could help explain dark energy ScienceDaily 科学

A Canadian radio telescope has detected faint hydrogen glow from billions of years ago using only its own observations, proving a long-awaited method for mapping the distant universe can work. CHIME can use this hydrogen signal to trace how matter is spread across space and measure cosmic expansion over time, offering a new way to study dark energy.

Read more → NASA astronauts splash down after 100 million miles in space ScienceDaily 科学

NASA’s SpaceX Crew-12 astronauts returned to Earth after 237 days in space, traveling more than 100 million miles and completing over 3,792 orbits while conducting research for future Moon and Mars missions. The mission also set spacewalk records and carried experiments that could transform medicine in space and on Earth.
