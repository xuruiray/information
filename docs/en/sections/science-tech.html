A famous quantum effect can finally be predicted in real materials ScienceDaily 技术

Scientists have developed a powerful new way to calculate the Kondo effect, a quantum phenomenon that appears when magnetic atoms are embedded in metals. Instead of simplified models, the method uses real atomic and electronic structures, allowing far more accurate predictions of material behavior. The approach could aid the design of quantum and electronic materials.

Scientific Research Briefing

This subsection covers fusion energy, nuclear clocks, neuromodulation and astrophysics. Princeton researchers propose a potentially more efficient route to fusion; a Vienna team builds the first self-stabilizing nuclear clock; an injectable device can wirelessly control nerves; and MIT astronomers find a star slowly devouring a brown dwarf. Together they reflect steady progress in both applied and fundamental science.

Scientists build a nuclear clock that could make atomic clocks obsolete ScienceDaily 科学

Researchers in Vienna have created the world’s first self-stabilizing nuclear clock, using thorium nuclei instead of electrons in atoms. Because nuclei are far less sensitive to outside disturbances, the technology could eventually surpass even the most precise atomic clocks. The advance marks a major step in timekeeping and could improve navigation, fundamental physics tests and other precision measurements.

Read more → Scientists created a tiny device that can control your nerves to treat chronic pain ScienceDaily 科学

Scientists have developed a tiny injectable device that can control nerve activity without surgery, batteries or wires, potentially transforming treatment for chronic pain and movement disorders. The seed-sized technology is powered wirelessly from outside the body and has shown promising early results. If further testing succeeds, it could offer a less invasive alternative to existing implants.

Unlike stars that rapidly swallow companions, this one steals material gradually over billions of years.
