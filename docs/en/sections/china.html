China Watch: Foreign Ministry Speaks on Taiwan, India and Cybersecurity as Governance Moves Advance Across Sectors

On October 13, Foreign Ministry spokesperson Mao Ning addressed several issues at a regular press conference: urging the United States to stop sending wrong signals to "Taiwan independence" separatist forces, firmly opposing India's publication of so-called "standard place names," and rejecting attempts to smear China over cybersecurity. In the same period, with State Council approval, the Ministry of Natural Resources and the National Development and Reform Commission released the Mineral Resources Catalog, listing 174 minerals and 119 sub-minerals. On industry and local affairs, an infographic was released for the Action Plan for Promoting New Energy Ships on Inland Waterways (2026-2030), and Hainan added four items to China's Important Agricultural Heritage list. On society and the rule of law, the Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs announced China's first Bryde's whale important habitat, covering 335.43 square kilometers with an annual 8.5-month fishing ban; police in Holingol, Inner Mongolia, busted a digital invoice fraud case involving nearly 1.9 billion yuan; Beijing police dismantled a gang providing voice relay services for overseas telecom fraud, exposing how local-number scam calls work; and Xiangyuan County in Shanxi shifted to data-driven smart enforcement.

First Bryde's Whale Habitat Established; Digital Invoice Fraud and Telecom Scam Relay Gang Uncovered

The Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs announced the establishment of the Bryde's whale important habitat off Weizhou Island in Beihai, Guangxi, covering 335.43 square kilometers with an annual 8.5-month fishing ban, filling a gap in protecting habitats of large offshore whales. Police in Holingol, Inner Mongolia, busted a major case of fraudulent digital electronic invoices involving 1.895 billion yuan in value and tax, the first such case in the region since fully digitalized electronic invoices were promoted. Beijing police dismantled a black-market gang providing voice relay services for overseas telecom fraud operations, where calls display local numbers but originate abroad; officers demonstrated the remote phone control process. Xiangyuan County in Shanxi has moved from experience-based to data-driven smart enforcement to protect its environment.
